Foundation

What is an electrical distribution system?

An electrical power system has three main stages — generation, transmission, and distribution. Energy is generated at power stations, transmitted over long distances at high voltage, and delivered to consumers through the distribution network.

The distribution system is the part of the power system that delivers energy from distribution substations to domestic, commercial, and industrial consumers. It includes feeders, distributors, service mains, transformers, and protective equipment.

Its objective is to provide a reliable, safe, and economical supply while keeping voltage within permissible limits.

Transmission vs distribution levels

StageTypical voltagePurpose
TransmissionHigh (kV – hundreds of kV)Bulk, long-distance transfer
Primary distributionMedium (e.g. 11 kV, 33 kV)Feeder from substation to area
Secondary distributionLow (e.g. 415 V, 230 V)Supply to consumers

Nominal values are illustrative. Actual voltage levels and network arrangements vary by utility and application.

Anatomy

Feeders, distributors & service mains

⚡

Feeders

Carry power from the distribution substation to distribution points. No consumer is tapped along the route. Sized primarily by current-carrying capacity and permissible voltage drop.

🔌

Distributors

Have consumer connections tapped along their length, so current varies along the line. Designed mainly to keep consumer terminal voltage within permissible limits.

🏠

Service mains

The final connection from a distributor to an individual consumer's premises. Sized by the consumer's load, current capacity, and voltage-drop limits.
